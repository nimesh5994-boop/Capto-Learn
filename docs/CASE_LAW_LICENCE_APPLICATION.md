12. **Main purpose (no more than 150 words; this is 146):**

> Capto Learn is a training and research service for UK accountancy and tax practitioners. We want to help staff find and correctly apply the tax case law behind HMRC's guidance. We will hold a copy of published tax judgments from the First-tier Tribunal (Tax), the Upper Tribunal (Tax and Chancery) and appeal courts. Practitioners can then search them alongside the official guidance and open the full judgment on Find Case Law. When a user asks a research question, our software retrieves relevant passages and quotes them word for word, with the neutral citation and a link to the authoritative judgment. Every quotation is checked automatically against the source text. The methods are full-text search, citation extraction (linking judgments to the HMRC manual paragraphs that cite them) and retrieval for answers that cite their sources. We will not profile individuals, predict outcomes or provide automated legal advice.

## Section 4: Public statement (about 150 words; this is 141)

> Capto Learn helps accountants and tax advisers understand the tax law they apply every day. Practitioners often meet a tribunal decision named in HMRC's guidance but struggle to find it and read what was actually decided. With this licence, Capto Learn will let subscribing firms search published tax judgments alongside the official guidance. Each result shows the case name, the neutral citation and the relevant passage, and links to the full judgment on The National Archives' Find Case Law. Our research assistant quotes judgments word for word, checks every quotation against the source, and always points readers to the authoritative judgment. We say clearly that the collection is incomplete and that our service is a research aid, not legal advice. We do not analyse or profile the individuals named in judgments, predict case outcomes, or let search engines index judgment text.

## Section 6: Nine principles

28. **Accept all nine principles as licence terms?** Yes

29. **How we will meet them** (paste each block under its heading):

**1. Dignity of the courts.**
- **Risk:** extracts taken out of context could misrepresent a decision.
- **Mitigation:** we quote judgments word for word, never paraphrase them as the court's words, and always give the neutral citation and a link to the full judgment. We don't rate judges, comment on judicial performance or rank decisions.

**2. Independence of the court.**
- **Risk:** analysis used to pressure or predict individual judges or panels.
- **Mitigation:** we don't analyse judges, panels or outcomes by decision-maker, and we don't predict decisions. The service supports understanding of the law as decided.

**3. Appropriate scrutiny.**
- **Risk:** conclusions drawn from an incomplete dataset; re-identification of individuals.
- **Mitigation:** results state the collection's coverage and limits. We don't extract or publish personal details beyond case names, don't combine judgment data with other datasets about individuals, and don't offer search by personal attributes, so jigsaw identification is not supported.

**4. Anti-discriminatory harm.**
- **Risk:** answers that consistently skew one way or disadvantage particular groups.
- **Mitigation:** we don't process or infer protected characteristics. Answer quality is monitored through our graded benchmark and user feedback (every answer can be rated and reported), and qualified reviewers investigate any pattern of misleading conclusions.

**5. Anti-bias.**
- **Risk:** bias entering retrieval or answering over time.
- **Mitigation:** governance through our editorial standards, a periodic accuracy benchmark graded by qualified reviewers, a firm-level review and approval register for research answers, and named reviewers accountable for content sign-off. Changes to retrieval or prompts are tested against the benchmark before release.

**6. Personal privacy.**
- **Risk:** processing personal data contained in judgments.
- **Mitigation:** we process judgments only to provide search and cited answers to subscribers, under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 (lawful basis: legitimate interests, with a documented assessment). Data is held in UK/EU-region infrastructure with access limited to our service. We'll honour data subject rights, remove or update records that Find Case Law removes or corrects, and keep no personal data beyond what appears in the published judgment. CONFIRM you'll complete a legitimate interests assessment and record this in your privacy notice.

**7. Discoverability.**
- **Risk:** judgment text becoming indexed by search engines or scraped from our service.
- **Mitigation:** judgment text and extracts are only available to logged-in subscribers. Case pages are excluded from search engines (noindex, and robots rules). We rate-limit and monitor access, and don't offer bulk export of judgment text or extracted data.

**8. Algorithmic transparency.**
- **Risk:** users not understanding how results and answers are produced.
- **Mitigation:** our editorial standards page explains the method: full-text search, citation linking, AI-written answers that must quote sources, and automatic word-for-word verification of every quotation. Each answer shows its sources, whether each quotation was verified, and a confidence level.

**9. Accurate data representation.**
- **Risk:** overstating findings, or relying on outdated or removed versions.
- **Mitigation:** our sync re-checks Find Case Law regularly (at least weekly) using its publication feeds, updates changed judgments and removes withdrawn ones. Every result links to the authoritative version, and answers state the dataset's limits rather than presenting results as exhaustive.
